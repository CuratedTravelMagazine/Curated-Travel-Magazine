<p>The Great Smoky Mountains may be Tennessee’s best-known autumn destination, but the state’s fall landscape extends well beyond its most visited national park. From East Tennessee’s mountain overlooks and scenic railways to Middle Tennessee’s waterfalls, historic parkways and harvest-colored plateaus, Tennessee is highlighting a collection of lesser-known places to experience the season. In the west, travelers can explore lakeside forests, botanical gardens and wildlife-rich wetlands, with opportunities ranging from kayaking and birdwatching to horseback riding and leisurely sunset drives.</p>
<p>Among the standout experiences are Roan Mountain State Park, where fall color typically peaks in mid-October and the night sky offers a different kind of spectacle; Fall Creek Falls State Park, home to dramatic waterfalls, extensive trails and overnight accommodations; and Reelfoot Lake State Park, where autumn scenery coincides with the arrival of migratory birds, including bald eagles. The Natchez Trace Parkway offers a particularly evocative road-trip route through Tennessee, while destinations such as Meeman-Shelby Forest State Park and Memphis Botanic Garden provide accessible ways to enjoy seasonal color near Memphis. Tennessee is also expanding access to the experience through colorblind viewfinders equipped with EnChroma lens technology at scenic locations across the state.</p>
<h3>The Curated Take</h3>
<p>Tennessee’s fall appeal lies in its variety. The state’s foliage can be experienced through a mountain hike, a quiet lake excursion, a historic road trip or a cultivated garden, allowing travelers to shape an autumn itinerary around their own pace and interests. The addition of colorblind viewfinders is especially meaningful, underscoring that the beauty of the season should be more accessible and inclusive rather than reserved for those who experience color in conventional ways.</p>
