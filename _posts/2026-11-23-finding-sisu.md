Ssss… as the ladle strikes the rocks. Cedar-scented steam rises in a sudden, fragrant cloud. Heat and sweat traces down the body. Winter seems impossibly far away. Until the door opens.

Cold air rushes in. Bare feet meet the frost. Steam rising from your skin as though your entire body has begun to exhale. Somewhere ahead lies the water, dark and glacial beneath the Finnish sky. The sensible instinct is to retreat. The challenge, should you choose to accept it, may be the biggest of your life. Or at least your vacation.

Welcome to the Finnish winter, where the art of warming up has a counterpart: the exhilarating, occasionally intimidating ritual of meeting the cold. Here, _sisu_ — that elusive Finnish quality of inner resolve, perseverance, and courage when comfort ends — takes on a new dimension. And across three very different landscapes, the water offers three ways to find it.  
  
The journey begins with Finnair, whose newly upgraded business-class seats transform the transatlantic crossing into an opportunity to settle in and arrive refreshed. With lie-flat comfort for the overnight flight, the Finnish winter feels a little closer before the plane even touches down in Helsinki.

### HELSINKI

#### The Urban Baltic Plunge

Helsinki wears winter beautifully. Along streets lined with ornate Art Nouveau facades, where mythical creatures and woodland motifs emerge from the stonework, Finland’s capital feels both sophisticated and quietly imaginative. At the Ateneum Art Museum, the country’s national art collection traces Finnish creativity from the 18th century through Modernism.

Afterward, head to the Old Market Hall (_Vanha Kauppahalli_) along the harbor. Inside this 1889 brick landmark, rich carved woodwork, narrow aisles, and cozy timber merchant stalls feel like an old-world sanctuary from the cold. Stepping up to one of the wooden counters offers a delicious introduction to local flavors—from steaming bowls of salmon soup topped with fresh dill to freshly baked rye specialties and cured Arctic fish.

For a change of scenery, board the ferry to Suomenlinna, the UNESCO World Heritage-listed sea fortress scattered across islands at the harbor entrance. Even in the depths of winter, its main paths remain clear for walking, where snow-dusted ramparts and windswept shorelines offer a dramatic view of the frozen Baltic.

As winter dusk settles over Helsinki, step into the world of Allas Sea Pool, where the Baltic meets the heart of the city. Climb down into the outdoor seawater pool and let the cold close around you, the water stretching toward the harbor while Helsinki rises behind it in a sweep of elegant waterfront buildings and glowing windows. Ferries cross the darkening sea; the last light catches the horizon before the city begins to glitter against the winter sky. The water can hover near freezing in the depths of winter, around 0–5°C (32–41°F), making the short distance from sauna to sea feel like a leap into another element entirely. Steam lifts from your skin as you surface, the skyline reflected in the rippling water, and the warm glow of the sauna beckons just steps away. At Allas, the city isn’t a view from a distant window. It surrounds you, while the Baltic itself becomes part of the experience.  
  
Afterward, warm up high above the harbor at **Palace**, Helsinki’s two-Michelin-starred restaurant, where contemporary Nordic cuisine unfolds against sweeping views of the Baltic. The tasting menu draws on the region’s ingredients with precision and imagination, moving from salted whitefish with yuzu and avocado to delicate vendace roe with Lappish potato and acidulated cream, before pike perch arrives with Oscietra caviar and sweet onions. Each course offers a different expression of northern flavors, a fitting counterpoint to the bracing simplicity of the sea below.

For a luxurious retreat, **The Hotel Maria** offers spacious suites, a restorative spa, and a quiet sense of occasion after a day exploring the capital. Helsinki rewards those who give it time, from its Art Nouveau streets and cultural treasures to its distinctive food scene and harbor-side rituals. Helsinki does not pretend the cold is decorative. It just puts the city next to it and lets you choose.

### IMATRA

#### Surrender on the Vuoksi River

In Finland’s Lakeland, forests gather around a vast network of lakes and waterways, and winter brings a quieter grandeur to the landscape. Near Imatra, the Vuoksi River cuts through the southeast, its flowing waters a striking counterpoint to the snow-covered banks. The region has long drawn visitors to its dramatic natural scenery, while the castle-like Imatran Valtionhotelli lends an air of old-world romance to the riverside.

Yet the most surprising way to experience this landscape is not from a scenic overlook, but from the water itself.

For dry-suit ice floating, participants slip into specially designed waterproof flotation suits before easing into the river. Warm and dry inside the suit, you lie back and float on the surface, carried gently by the current. The sensation is less a battle with the cold than a total surrender to the water: the winter sky overhead, snow-laden pine forests drifting past, and an unexpected feeling of weightlessness. Unlike a direct plunge, ice floating removes the shock of icy water while offering an intimate encounter with the river. It is an unusual expression of _sisu_ — the willingness to trust an unfamiliar experience and let the landscape take the lead. For those seeking the classic thermal shock, traditional lakeside saunas offer the chance to step out in a swimsuit for a direct plunge into carved lake ice.

### ROVANIEMI

#### The Arctic Lake & Celestial Sky

Beyond the Arctic Circle, winter takes on an almost otherworldly scale. At Apukka Resort, outside Rovaniemi, forests frame Lake Olkkajärvi, and the long northern nights invite travelers to look skyward. Glass-roofed igloos offer the possibility of watching for the Northern Lights from the warmth of your bed; when conditions align, their luminous green ribbons dance directly above the wilderness.

Beyond aurora gazing, the High North invites a slower immersion into the landscape. By day, visitors can trek on snowshoes through snow-heavy pine forests where deep winter silence settles over the trees, or explore local Arctic culture at the Arktikum Museum. In the evenings, private dining inside heated glass domes serves elevated Nordic cuisine surrounded by the dark forest canopy.

At the resort’s lakeside sauna, the contrast between warmth and cold becomes wonderfully immediate. Water thrown onto the hot stones releases a cloud of steam, while beyond the door Lake Olkkajärvi waits beneath the Arctic night. Apukka offers guided sauna experiences paired with a deliberate cold plunge into an ice hole cut straight into the lake. The descent down the ladder is calm and deliberate; the cold is real, and the return to the warmth of the fireside is deeply restorative. Here, _sisu_ is less about conquering the Arctic than meeting it with curiosity, respect, and a willingness to step beyond the comfort of the known.
