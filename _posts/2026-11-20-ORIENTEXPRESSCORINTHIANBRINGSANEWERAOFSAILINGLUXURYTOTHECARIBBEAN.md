The 220-meter sailing yacht will connect destinations including the British Virgin Islands, Anguilla, Saint Martin, Saint-Barthélemy, Antigua, Barbuda, Barbados and Puerto Rico through voyages designed to make the passage itself part of the experience. Its January itinerary from Saint Martin to San Juan, Puerto Rico, includes stops in St. Kitts and Nevis, Antigua, Anguilla and Virgin Gorda, home to the celebrated Baths. Designed by architect Maxime d’Angeac, the yacht features 54 suites with panoramic sea views and dedicated butler service, five restaurants overseen by 18-Michelin-starred chef Yannick Alléno, a Guerlain Spa, eight bars and a marina. Three SolidSail rigs, supported by hybrid LNG propulsion, bring an innovative approach to wind-powered luxury travel.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Caribbean Sea. Courtesy of Orient Express.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>There is a distinction between traveling through the Caribbean and experiencing it from the sea. A sailing journey offers a changing perspective on the region, connecting islands and coastlines through the passages between them rather than treating each destination as an isolated stop. With its combination of expansive suites, fine dining and advanced sailing technology, Orient Express Corinthian brings the romance of ocean travel into a contemporary luxury setting. Its arrival also signals a new chapter for the Caribbean’s luxury cruise landscape, where the vessel itself becomes as significant as the destinations along the way.</p>
