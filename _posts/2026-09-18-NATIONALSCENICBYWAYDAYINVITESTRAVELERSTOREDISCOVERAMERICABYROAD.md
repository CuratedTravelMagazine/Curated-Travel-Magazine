<p>September 22 will mark the first annual National Scenic Byway Day, a new observance encouraging travelers to explore the scenic, historic and culturally significant roads that connect communities across the United States. The timing is particularly notable in 2026, which also marks the nation’s 250th birthday and the centennial of the U.S. Numbered Highway System. For travelers drawn to road trips, the day offers a reason to slow down, leave the major highways behind and experience the places and stories found along America’s scenic routes.</p>
<p>Among the country’s standout byways is the 444-mile Natchez Trace Parkway, stretching through Mississippi, Alabama and Tennessee. The route combines sweeping landscapes with archaeological sites, historic markers, recreational opportunities and access to small communities, tracing layers of history from Indigenous homelands and early travel routes to the modern road trip. Travelers can mark National Scenic Byway Day by planning a scenic drive, visiting a byway community, exploring local museums and historic sites, or building a longer itinerary around one of America’s designated scenic routes.</p>
<h3>The Curated Take</h3>
<p>There is something especially appealing about a travel day built around the idea that getting there can be part of the experience. Scenic byways offer a slower form of American travel, where landscapes, local history and small communities become part of the itinerary rather than scenery passed at highway speed. National Scenic Byway Day arrives as a timely invitation to rediscover that kind of journey.</p>
