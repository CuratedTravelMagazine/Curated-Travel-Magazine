<p>I didn’t set out to create a magazine. Not really.</p>
<p>One week, I sold $1 million in advertising for the company I worked for. The next, I was laid off for “geopolitical reasons.” In every interview that followed, I heard some variation of the same question: I see you’re analytical, but are you creative? Saying yes was one thing; putting the work on the page was another. So I decided to create something tangible.</p>
<p>I would publish three issues of a travel magazine showcasing my ability to write, edit, and design. Three issues. That was the plan.</p>
<p>But by the time I reached the third, the audience had grown, the responses were rolling in, and I knew I wasn’t going to turn back. Every issue became a love letter...to the industry I spent 20 years promoting, to the art of traveling well, and to the people and places that change us—written with the luxury traveler in mind.</p>
<p>Early in the life of the magazine, I found myself in Miami, a city I’ve visited more times than I can count. But I have a habit when I travel: even in places I know well, I want to find something I haven’t seen before. So I wandered a little farther from the familiar, found a charming little courtyard, ordered a mocktail and settled in to do one of my favorite things—watch people.</p>
<p>And somewhere between the clink of glasses, the passing conversations and the rhythm of the afternoon, it hit me: <em>this is me.</em></p>
<p>Not that I didn’t already know I loved travel. I had spent 20 years building a career around it. But this felt different. Deeper. The kind of knowing you don’t arrive at through a résumé or a job title. I could feel it in my bones.</p>
<p>Maybe that was what Curated Travel had been becoming all along: not simply a magazine about where to go, but an invitation to look a little closer once you get there.</p>
<p>I stayed up past midnight planning layouts and narrative arcs, researching destinations, and obsessing over the details. I pored over other magazines for inspiration—not just travel, but fashion, food, and, yes, even a decidedly unexpected fishing magazine that somehow made it into the stack.</p>
<p>A year, seven issues, and a frankly absurd amount of iced coffee later, the road hasn’t been without its hurdles.</p>
<p>One of the early hurdles was structural. A major industry platform required a specific format for access. Instead of reshaping the magazine to fit, I built a parallel channel that met the requirement while keeping the editorial standard intact. That channel later became the main route for syndication.</p>
<p>That moment was the pivot point. The accidental experiment stopped being finite and started feeling like a place with its own center of gravity.</p>
<p>I love curating the destination mix, finding the hotels worth highlighting, discovering the stories hiding beneath a place’s obvious attractions—and hearing what you think of the world we’re building here. I’ve learned that some of the best narratives begin in the most unexpected places.<br/><br/>Before you cross the street, you look both ways. So, on this anniversary, I’m taking a moment to look back at where we started—and then forward to wherever we’re headed. To everyone who has subscribed, read, shared, recommended, and wandered through these pages: thank you. You’ve made this possible.</p>
<p><strong>Now, let’s see where we curate next.<br/><br/></strong></p>
