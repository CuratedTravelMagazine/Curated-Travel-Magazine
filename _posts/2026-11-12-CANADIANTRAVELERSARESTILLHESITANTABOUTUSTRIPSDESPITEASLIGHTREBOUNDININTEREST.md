<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/321500a6-1376-469d-b3a4-42779c71a16a_600x336.jpeg"/></figure><p>Canadian travel to the United States remains below its earlier levels, according to the latest October 2026 tracking study from Longwoods International. The survey found that 31% of Canadian travelers had visited the U.S. in the previous six months, little changed from a year earlier, while 18% had canceled planned trips. More notably, the share reporting no U.S. travel during that period rose to 51%, compared with 47% a year ago. There are signs of a potential recovery, however, with 47% of respondents saying they intend to visit the United States within the next 12 months, up from 39% in April 2025. Among those not currently planning a trip, 70% said an improved political relationship between Canada and the U.S. would make them more likely to visit, while 40% pointed to a more favorable exchange rate. The findings highlight the influence of cross-border relations, travel costs and currency fluctuations on Canadian tourism demand, a significant consideration for U.S. destinations and hospitality businesses that depend on visitors from their northern neighbor.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Longwoods International</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>The Canadian market remains an important part of the U.S. tourism landscape, and these findings suggest that rebuilding traveler confidence will require more than destination marketing alone. Political sentiment, affordability and the value of the Canadian dollar are shaping decisions about where and when to travel. The increase in stated intentions for the year ahead offers some encouragement, but the growing share of travelers who have recently stayed away is a reminder that destinations cannot take this neighboring audience for granted. For U.S. hotels, tourism boards and travel businesses, the opportunity lies in understanding what is keeping Canadian visitors at home and addressing the factors that could bring them back.</p>
