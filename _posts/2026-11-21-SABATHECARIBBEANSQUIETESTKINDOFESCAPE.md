<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/7c5798e2-79ed-4ffa-9330-dd890b47add8_4249x2835.jpeg"/></figure><p>Just five square miles in size, with a population of approximately 2,000, Saba offers a distinctly different perspective on Caribbean travel. Known as the “Unspoiled Queen of the Caribbean,” the small Dutch Caribbean island has resisted large-scale development, with just five hotels and approximately 140 guest rooms preserving a sense of intimacy rarely found elsewhere in the region. Its dramatic volcanic landscape encompasses 20 hiking trails, five distinct microclimates, and Mount Scenery, the highest point in the Kingdom of the Netherlands at 887 meters. Trails wind through lush rainforest, cloud-covered mountainsides, and changing landscapes, while the surrounding waters are protected as a marine park. In Windwardside, a charming village retains a strong local character, offering another way to experience an island whose appeal rests as much in its atmosphere as its natural beauty.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Saba, Caribbean Netherlands. Courtesy of Saba Tourism Bureau.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>Saba’s distinction is not simply that it is small, but that it has preserved the qualities that make a place feel like itself. There are no sprawling resort developments competing for attention, only a remarkable landscape, a close-knit community, and the freedom to experience the Caribbean at a different pace. For travelers accustomed to destinations defined by their amenities and activity, Saba offers another kind of discovery: the pleasure of finding a place where nature sets the rhythm and the absence of crowds becomes a luxury in its own right.</p>
