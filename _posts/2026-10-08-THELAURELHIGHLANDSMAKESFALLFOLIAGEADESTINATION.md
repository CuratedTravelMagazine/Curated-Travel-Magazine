<p>Pennsylvania’s Laurel Highlands has earned a place among the country’s standout fall foliage destinations for the seventh consecutive year, placing sixth in the 2026 USA Today 10Best Readers’ Choice Awards for Best Destination for Fall Colors. Set across the Allegheny Mountains, the region typically reaches peak color around mid-October, when its mountain ridges and river valleys take on layers of red, gold, and orange. Nearly 200,000 acres of public lands, along with hiking and biking trails including the Great Allegheny Passage, give visitors plenty of ways to experience the landscape beyond a scenic drive.</p>
<p>The season also brings a distinctly local rhythm to the Laurel Highlands, with festivals and attractions providing reasons to linger. Confluence PumpkinFest, Fort Ligonier Days, Delmont Apple ‘n Arts Festival, and Seven Springs Autumnfest fill the September and October calendar, while the region’s historic sites and architectural landmarks offer another dimension to an autumn visit. Fallingwater, Kentuck Knob, and other Frank Lloyd Wright works, along with Ohiopyle State Park and the area’s mountain resorts, make the foliage backdrop part of a broader cultural and outdoor itinerary.</p>
<p><strong>The Curated Take:</strong> The Laurel Highlands is most interesting in fall when the foliage becomes a gateway rather than the entire experience. The combination of mountain landscapes, Frank Lloyd Wright architecture, historic sites, local festivals, craft beverages, and outdoor recreation gives visitors reasons to build an itinerary around the season rather than simply pass through in search of peak color.</p>
