<p>Bellevue, Washington, is making it easier to experience the Pacific Northwest without a car, with a growing network of shuttles, light rail connections and bike options linking travelers from arrival to outdoor adventure. The expanded Bellevue Airport &amp; Cruise Shuttle connects hotels with Seattle-Tacoma International Airport and Seattle’s cruise terminals, while the new Bellevue Light Rail Shuttle provides free, on-demand electric transportation from the Bellevue Downtown Station to hotels, restaurants, shops and attractions. Ride Bellevue adds another layer, giving visitors access to e-bikes as well as gravel and mountain bikes for exploring the region without bringing their own equipment.</p>
<p>The transportation changes arrive as Bellevue adds new reasons to make the city a base for a Pacific Northwest trip. Nobu Bellevue and Nobu Bellevue Residences are planned for 2027, alongside The Emily Hotel and a new winter festival centered on light, art and seasonal programming. In the Wilburton district, the evolving Eastrail corridor and restoration of the historic Wilburton Trestle will connect visitors to a growing regional trail network. With more than 100 parks and public spaces, Lake Washington shoreline and easy connections to Seattle, Woodinville Wine Country and mountain recreation, Bellevue is positioning itself as a place where urban comfort and the outdoors can be part of the same itinerary.</p>
<p><strong>The Curated Take:</strong> Bellevue’s most interesting development may be the connective tissue between its experiences. Rather than requiring travelers to choose between a polished urban stay and access to the Pacific Northwest landscape, the city is building a model around both. For travelers who prefer the ease of a sophisticated home base but still want nature, culture and regional exploration within reach, that changes how Bellevue fits into a Seattle-area itinerary.</p>
