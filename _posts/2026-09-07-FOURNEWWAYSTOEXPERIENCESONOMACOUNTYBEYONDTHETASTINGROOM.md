<p>Wine may remain central to Sonoma County’s identity, but some of the destination’s newest experiences are looking elsewhere, toward food, agriculture, art and the redwoods.</p>
<p>In downtown Petaluma, Taste Petaluma brings four local concepts together under one roof: Barber Cellars, Petaluma Cheese Shop, Barber Lee Spirits and Nightcap Dessert Bar. Guests can move from Sonoma County wines and craft cocktails to artisan cheeses, chocolates and desserts in a relaxed lounge setting. In Santa Rosa, Gold Ridge Organic Farms is opening Heritage Apple Press, a 20,000-square-foot facility designed to process fruit from small and family-owned farms, helping preserve the region’s apple-growing heritage while supporting fresh-packed apples, juice, cider and vinegar.</p>
<p>Art is also becoming part of the resort experience. Fairmont Sonoma Mission Inn &amp; Spa has debuted the Lori Austin Gallery at the Fairmont, a rotating pop-up gallery showcasing contemporary work by regional artists. And for travelers who would rather head into the trees, Sonoma Zipline Adventures is expanding with Redwood Aerial Adventure, a 40,000-square-foot ropes and challenge course expected to open in spring 2027, with aerial routes, sky bridges and a separate treetop play area for younger visitors.</p>
<p><strong>The Curated Take:</strong> The appeal here is variety. Sonoma County can still be a wine destination, but travelers increasingly want something to do between tastings, whether that means discovering local producers, seeing regional art or climbing into a redwood canopy. These experiences give visitors more reasons to slow down and engage with the place itself.</p>
