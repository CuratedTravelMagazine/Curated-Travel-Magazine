<p>Each winter, the waters of Samaná Bay in the Dominican Republic become the stage for one of the Caribbean’s most remarkable natural spectacles. From January through March, roughly 2,500 humpback whales return to the warm tropical waters to mate and give birth, bringing an extraordinary seasonal rhythm to the bay. At Cayo Levantado Resort, a secluded retreat on its own island within Samaná Bay, guests can experience the migration through seasonal whale-watching excursions, with opportunities to observe the whales breaching, surfacing and interacting with their calves in their natural habitat.</p>
<p>The migration is woven into the identity of the resort itself. Its open-air wellness sanctuary, Yubarta, takes its name from the humpback whale that returns to Samaná each year. Surrounded by tropical gardens, the sanctuary is dedicated to meditation, movement, rituals and personal reflection, creating a thoughtful connection between the whales’ annual return and the idea of reconnecting with one’s own natural rhythms. Beyond the migration, winter guests can kayak and paddleboard through turquoise waters, explore the island’s tropical landscape, relax on private beaches and venture into nearby Los Haitises National Park.</p>
<p><strong>The Curated Take:</strong> The most compelling aspect of winter in Samaná is that the season has its own reason for being there. The whales transform the bay for several months each year, and Cayo Levantado provides a particularly intimate vantage point from which to experience that natural cycle. Rather than simply offering warm-weather refuge, the resort becomes a base for experiencing a specific moment in the life of the destination.</p>
