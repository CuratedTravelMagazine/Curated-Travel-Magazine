<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/2330f8c7-6b4a-4561-86ef-8e09d8719a28_2880x3600.jpeg"/></figure><p>On Nosy Komba, off Madagascar’s northwest coast, Time + Tide Tsara Komba offers an intimate island stay shaped by its relationship with the neighboring community. The eight-room retreat shares its shoreline with a local village, creating opportunities for guests to experience daily life beyond the property. Guided walks and traditional dugout canoe journeys lead to Ampangorina and its market, while encounters with local fishing traditions offer a closer understanding of skills passed down through generations. Guests can also learn palangrotte fishing from a local guide, using a nylon line wrapped around a wooden plate in a technique that reflects the enduring connection between the island’s communities and the sea. The experience brings a cultural dimension to a stay in Madagascar’s Indian Ocean landscape, where the appeal extends beyond tropical scenery to the people and practices that give the island its character.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Nosy Komba, Madagascar. Courtesy of Time + Tide Tsara Komba and Andy Macdonald</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>The most meaningful island experiences often begin where the resort ends. On Nosy Komba, the opportunity to travel by dugout canoe, visit a village market and learn a traditional fishing technique offers a more grounded understanding of Madagascar. It is a reminder that a destination’s character is found not only in its landscapes, but in the knowledge and traditions sustained by the communities who call it home.</p>
