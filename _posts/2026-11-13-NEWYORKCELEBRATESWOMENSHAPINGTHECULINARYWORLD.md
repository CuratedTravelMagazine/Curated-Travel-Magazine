<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/73ed2ed5-ead0-4669-a9ec-eb382b30a925_600x596.jpeg"/></figure><p>For five decades, Les Dames d’Escoffier New York has championed the women shaping the worlds of food, wine and hospitality through education, mentorship and philanthropy. The founding and largest chapter of Les Dames d’Escoffier International marks its 50th anniversary in 2026 with a gala on November 9 at The Lighthouse at Pier Sixty in New York City, honoring celebrated chef, restaurateur and television personality Lidia Bastianich. The evening brings together an impressive collection of culinary talent, including Daniel Boulud, Eric Ripert, Marcus Samuelsson and Jacques Torres, alongside a series of mentor-and-mentee pairings that spotlight the importance of supporting the next generation of women in the industry. Since its founding in 1976, the organization has awarded more than 1,000 scholarships and distributed over $2 million in educational support, while building a professional community spanning chefs, restaurateurs, sommeliers, farmers, writers, educators and hospitality leaders. The anniversary celebrates not only its history, but also the women whose expertise and leadership continue to influence the culinary landscape.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: New York City, New York. Courtesy of Les Dames d’Escoffier New York.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>Food travel is often framed around celebrated chefs, acclaimed restaurants and memorable dining experiences, but the people shaping those experiences deserve recognition, too. Les Dames d’Escoffier New York offers a glimpse into the broader community of women whose work influences culinary culture, hospitality, wine, education and the future of food. Its 50-year milestone is particularly meaningful because it celebrates both achievement and the importance of opening doors for those who follow. In a field where mentorship and the exchange of knowledge help preserve traditions while encouraging innovation, that legacy reaches well beyond the kitchen.</p>
