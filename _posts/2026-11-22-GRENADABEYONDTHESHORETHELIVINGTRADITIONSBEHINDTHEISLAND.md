<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/8187d8ae-4a32-43f6-846f-8b155957f55b_3500x2335.jpeg"/></figure><p>At Six Senses La Sagesse, discovering Grenada means looking beyond its beaches to the people, landscapes, and traditions that give the island its character. A collection of locally rooted experiences invites guests into a more personal encounter with Grenadian life, beginning with Harvest Homestead, a visit to a two-acre organic farm led by Theresa Marryshow, head of Grenada’s local women’s farming organization. Guests learn about sustainable agriculture and the island’s food traditions while gaining insight into the work of local women farmers. The Hidden Haven excursion pairs a journey to the Seven Sisters Waterfalls with wellness and culinary elements, while the Reggae Fields Journey introduces guests to Grenadian cocoa farmers and beekeepers before inviting them to make their own cocoa balls. Together, these experiences reveal an island whose richness is found not only in its natural beauty, but in the knowledge, livelihoods, and cultural practices sustained by its communities.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: La Sagesse, Grenada. Courtesy of Six Senses La Sagesse.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>There is a particular depth to travel that makes room for the people who know a place best. On Grenada, a farm visit, a conversation with a beekeeper, or the making of a traditional cocoa treat can offer a more lasting connection than simply passing through a beautiful landscape. These encounters give the island’s natural appeal a human dimension, revealing the quiet richness of traditions carried forward through everyday life.</p>
