Among the additions is a journey through Brittany aboard the six-passenger Nymphea, tracing the River Oust and Canal de Nantes à Brest through medieval villages, historic châteaux and the region’s distinctive culinary landscape. In Burgundy, new visits to Château de Germolles and Château de Bazoches bring guests closer to medieval history through private falconry encounters, while the Canal du Midi itinerary introduces a tasting and private lunch at a family-owned wine and olive estate. Along Ireland’s River Shannon, guests can explore the heritage of Irish whiskey at Tullamore D.E.W. Distillery and the historic Birr Castle estate, including its remarkable scientific collections. With intimate vessels accommodating six to 20 guests, European Waterways combines gourmet dining, regional wines, personalized service and privately guided excursions for a slower, more immersive way to experience Europe.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Europe’s inland waterways. Courtesy of European Waterways.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>The appeal of hotel barging lies in its ability to make the journey itself part of the destination. Rather than moving quickly between Europe’s familiar landmarks, guests encounter smaller villages, family estates, historic residences and regional traditions at a more considered pace. The new experiences for 2027 add depth to that proposition, particularly through access that feels personal rather than transactional. A private encounter at a château, lunch among the vineyards or a closer look at Ireland’s scientific heritage can offer a richer understanding of a region than simply passing through it. For travelers drawn to cultural discovery, exceptional food and the intimacy of small-ship travel, Europe’s waterways remain an especially compelling way to explore.</p>
