<p>Namron Hospitality is expanding its presence in Mexico with two distinctly intimate additions: <strong>MUSA</strong>, a 177-acre regenerative retreat on Guerrero’s Pacific coast near Zihuatanejo, and <strong>NEST Los Cabos</strong>, a six-accommodation desert oasis in Baja California Sur. While the properties occupy very different landscapes, both are designed around the same premise: luxury hospitality that works with its surroundings rather than overwhelming them.</p>
<p>At MUSA, 13 Hotelito rooms, nine Hideaway casitas, four Basecamp rooms, five Casa Musa beach houses and seven villas are spread across an oceanfront landscape backed by the Sierra Madre del Sur. Guests can move between a natural biopool, working farm, apiary, turtle sanctuary, wildlife reserves, historic salt mines, art galleries and a beachfront club, with regenerative farming, tree planting and artist residencies adding an experiential dimension. At NEST Los Cabos, a former private home has been transformed into a six-room retreat within the Santiago oasis, where palm groves, waterfalls and natural hot springs frame kayaking, hiking, seasonal dining and wellness experiences. Both properties emphasize local sourcing, community employment and environmental stewardship.</p>
<h3>The Curated Take</h3>
<p>Namron’s expansion is notable less for its size than for where it is choosing to grow. MUSA and NEST Los Cabos represent a version of Mexican luxury that is deliberately low-density and deeply tied to place, whether that means a working regenerative landscape on the Pacific coast or a secluded oasis in the Sierra de la Laguna. For travelers looking beyond Mexico’s established resort corridors, these properties offer a compelling argument for going somewhere quieter.</p>
