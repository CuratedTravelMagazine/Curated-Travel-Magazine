<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/96560c0e-2614-46c7-844d-06e8f9b132a1.jpeg"/></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0878a51f-b150-41f7-88e2-f705689ede34_2752x1536.jpeg"/></figure><p>Japanese art collective <strong>teamLab</strong> is bringing its immersive art experience to Anaheim, California, with plans for its first permanent U.S. museum at OCVIBE in 2030. The approximately 110,000-square-foot museum will feature interactive installations that respond to visitors, making people an active part of the artwork rather than simply observers. Designed by Smith-Clementi, the museum will be part of OCVIBE, a developing arts, entertainment, dining, and hospitality district near the Honda Center.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: teamLab / OCVIBE. Architectural rendering of the planned museum in Anaheim, California.</figcaption></figure><p><strong>The Curated Take</strong></p><p>For travelers who have experienced teamLab’s celebrated installations in Japan, this brings a new opportunity closer to home. For California, it adds a distinctive cultural attraction to a destination increasingly bringing art, entertainment, and hospitality together. The larger appeal is the chance to experience art as something immersive and participatory, not simply something viewed from a distance.</p>
