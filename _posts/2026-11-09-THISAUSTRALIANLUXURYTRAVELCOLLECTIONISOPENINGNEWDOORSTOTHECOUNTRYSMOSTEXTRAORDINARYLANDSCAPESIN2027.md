Members of Luxury Lodges of Australia are preparing a new chapter of openings, refurbishments, private accommodations, and small-ship expeditions throughout 2027. Orpheus Island Lodge has already reopened following an extensive refurbishment, while Saffire Freycinet will introduce Saffire Jewel, an expansive private villa designed for families and small groups, in December 2026. Further ahead, Sal Salis Ningaloo Reef is scheduled to reopen in March 2027 with refreshed eco-luxury wilderness tents and guest spaces, followed by the May reopening of Bullo River Station after a major redevelopment of its remote outback homestead. May also marks the 40th anniversary of True North Adventure Cruises, which will introduce new expeditions exploring Western Australia’s Rowley Shoals and the Great Barrier Reef. Together, these developments offer fresh reasons to plan a luxury Australia itinerary built around extraordinary landscapes, private guiding, marine encounters, and access to places far beyond the usual travel routes.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Luxury Lodges of Australia, Australia. Courtesy of Luxury Lodges of Australia.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>Australia’s most compelling luxury experiences often begin where conventional travel becomes more difficult. What distinguishes this collection is not simply the standard of its accommodations, but the access each property provides to a particular landscape, community, or ecosystem. From the marine wilderness of Ningaloo and the Great Barrier Reef to Tasmania’s dramatic coastline and the vast cattle country of the Kimberley, these investments reinforce the appeal of journeys shaped by place rather than a checklist of destinations. For travelers considering Australia in 2027, the developments also create an opportunity to combine several distinct lodge experiences into one richly varied journey.</p>
