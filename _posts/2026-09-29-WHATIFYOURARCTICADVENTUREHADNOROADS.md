<p>On Andørja, one of Northern Europe’s most mountainous islands, Norrøna is creating a new kind of Arctic basecamp. Opening in June 2027, the <strong>Norrøna Lodge at Andørja</strong> will place travelers on one of Norway’s most remote coastlines, where 20 peaks rise more than 1,000 meters from the fjords and many are accessible only from the water.</p>
<p>The 15-cabin lodge will accommodate up to 30 guests and was designed to preserve the landscape rather than overpower it. Built around a former basking shark research center, the property uses timber construction, elevated structures and boardwalks, with no road access on site. Guests will arrive by boat, weather permitting, and stays will include guiding, dining, transportation and recovery. Depending on the season, experiences range from backcountry skiing, hiking, climbing and trail running beneath the midnight sun to northern lights excursions, snowshoeing and fishing. Local guides will also introduce guests to Andørja’s fishing traditions and communities, while wood-fired saunas, outdoor hot tubs and fjord dips provide a slower counterpoint to the adventure.</p>
<p><strong>The Curated Take:</strong> Norrøna is making an interesting leap from creating equipment for outdoor exploration to creating the destinations themselves. At Andørja, that philosophy is particularly compelling because the lodge is designed to make the landscape more accessible without turning it into a conventional resort. With locally sourced Arctic ingredients such as king crab, sea urchin, berries and wild herbs complementing the experience, this is adventure travel built around immersion rather than simply activity. The lodge will also serve as the first destination in Norrøna’s planned Arctic Voyage, a growing network intended to connect some of Norway’s most untouched coastline.</p>
