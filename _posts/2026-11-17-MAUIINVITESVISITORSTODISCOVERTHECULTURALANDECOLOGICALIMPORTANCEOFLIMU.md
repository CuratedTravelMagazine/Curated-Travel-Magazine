<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/dec1f958-3440-45f7-ad43-0ae00dee95af_600x450.jpeg"/></figure><p>An often-overlooked part of Hawaiʻi’s coastal environment takes center stage at Maui Ocean Center, where a new exhibit introduces visitors to limu, the diverse marine algae traditionally gathered and used throughout the islands. Opening November 12, the exhibit currently features four varieties of limu, with plans to expand the collection and eventually develop seed stock to support restoration projects around Maui. The opening celebration will bring together cultural and community organizations, including Ke Kīpuka Limu and Waimānalo Limu Hui, for hands-on educational activities exploring the ecological and cultural significance of these marine plants. Visitors can also experience limu-inspired food offerings and attend an evening educational program featuring specialists and aquaculturists. The initiative extends beyond the aquarium, with a community restoration project planned for Lāhainā the following day. Through the exhibit, Maui Ocean Center offers another perspective on Hawaiʻi’s marine environment, connecting coastal ecology, traditional knowledge and ongoing conservation efforts.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Maui, Hawaiʻi. Courtesy of Maui Ocean Center.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>Understanding a destination often begins with looking more closely at what lies beneath the familiar attractions. Limu is woven into Hawaiian foodways, cultural practices and the health of nearshore ecosystems, making this exhibit an opportunity to explore a meaningful part of the islands’ natural and cultural heritage. For visitors to Maui, it adds depth to an ocean-focused itinerary, moving beyond the beauty of the coastline to consider the knowledge, relationships and restoration work that help sustain it.</p>
