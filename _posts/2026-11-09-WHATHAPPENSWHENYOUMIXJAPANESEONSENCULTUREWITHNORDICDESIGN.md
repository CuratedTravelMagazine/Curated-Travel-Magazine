<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/d9863726-9578-4916-9431-99589b9e0edd_600x549.jpeg"/></figure><p>Copenhagen is about to welcome a distinctive new waterfront destination as KAI, a Japanese-inspired water culture house designed by Kengo Kuma &amp; Associates, prepares to open on December 5, 2026. Located on Paper Island in the Danish capital’s inner harbor, the striking new attraction brings together ten pools, saunas, steam bathing, cultural spaces, and year-round outdoor swimming. Its standout feature is a heated onsen overlooking the harbor, offering a Japanese approach to bathing against the backdrop of Copenhagen’s evolving waterfront. The architecture is equally distinctive, with five brick towers constructed from 300,000 handmade bricks rising above a light-filled glass structure. Inside, visitors will find everything from family-friendly pools and fitness facilities to warm-water therapy and cold plunges, while an outdoor pool nestled between the towers invites bathing beneath the open sky. Additional harbor swimming areas and a timber waterfront deck are expected to follow in early 2027, further connecting the development to the city’s public spaces and maritime heritage.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: KAI Water Culture House, Paper Island, Copenhagen, Denmark. Courtesy of KAI.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>Copenhagen has long understood how to make its waterfront part of everyday city life, and KAI adds an intriguing new dimension to that relationship. Rather than treating wellness as something reserved for a resort, the project brings bathing, architecture, and cultural gathering into the heart of the city. Its combination of Japanese onsen inspiration and Danish design creates a distinctive experience for visitors, while its public character reflects Copenhagen’s broader commitment to accessible urban spaces. For travelers planning a winter city break, KAI offers another reason to linger in the Danish capital beyond its celebrated restaurants, museums, and design scene.</p>
