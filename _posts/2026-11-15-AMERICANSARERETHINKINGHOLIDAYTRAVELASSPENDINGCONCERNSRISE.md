<figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/9d03b5b6-5e31-41fc-99d8-f217e078ec19_600x337.jpeg"/></figure><p>American holiday travel plans are showing signs of a slowdown, according to the latest Longwoods International tracking study, with more travelers expecting to cut back on both trips and spending in 2026. The October survey found that 31% of American travelers plan to travel less this holiday season than they did last year, an increase of eight percentage points from 2025 and 13 points from 2024. Meanwhile, 35% expect to spend less on holiday travel, up 11 points from the previous year, while only 16% anticipate traveling more and 22% expect to increase their spending. The broader outlook is also softening: the share of Americans with travel plans for the next six months has declined from 93% a year ago to 86%. Inflation and gasoline prices remain significant considerations, cited by 35% of respondents as major influences on their travel decisions. The survey, conducted October 6–9 among 1,000 U.S. adults, suggests that economic pressures are increasingly shaping how Americans approach the holiday travel season, although changes in fuel prices, airfares and consumer confidence could still influence demand.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0fa81f76-8bad-4f8c-b4ac-39ae46a44732_1200x500.jpeg"/><figcaption>Photo Credit: Courtesy of Longwoods International.</figcaption></figure><h3>The Curated Take</h3><p>The findings point to a holiday season in which travel decisions may be less about abandoning travel altogether and more about reassessing its cost and value. For the luxury travel industry, that distinction matters. Travelers who remain committed to meaningful experiences may become more selective about where they go, how long they stay and what makes a journey worth the investment. Destinations and hospitality brands will need to communicate value beyond the rate itself, emphasizing distinctive experiences, thoughtful service and the reasons a trip deserves a place in the holiday budget. The coming months will reveal whether this early softness becomes a broader pullback or a more deliberate approach to travel spending.</p>
