<p>Behind a weathered red gate in Santa Fe’s Barrio de Analco, The Inn of The Five Graces has unveiled Red Door, a private two-bedroom villa set within the historic Roque Tudesqui House. The approximately 2,000-square-foot residence offers guests the rare experience of staying inside a centuries-old adobe structure, with two private courtyards, a back deck, an expansive living room, dining room, kitchenette and richly appointed bedrooms. The house predates the Spanish colonial adobe era, with portions built using puddled adobe, an Indigenous construction technique that predates the introduction of Spanish-style adobe bricks.</p>
<p>Inside, Red Door reflects the distinctive design language of The Inn of The Five Graces, pairing historic architecture with rare antiques, custom textiles and intricate hand-laid mosaics collected from around the world. The villa’s secluded setting gives it the atmosphere of a private Santa Fe residence while retaining access to the inn’s hospitality and amenities. Adding another layer to the experience, the property’s celebrated wisteria, which cascades into purple blooms each June, is protected by a permanent preservation easement.</p>
<h3>The Curated Take</h3>
<p>Red Door is compelling precisely because the luxury here is inseparable from a sense of place. Rather than recreating Santa Fe’s history as décor, the villa places guests inside a building that has witnessed centuries of the city’s evolution. For travelers drawn to architecture, design and culturally layered stays, it offers something increasingly rare: the chance to experience Santa Fe not simply as a destination, but from within one of its historic homes.</p>
