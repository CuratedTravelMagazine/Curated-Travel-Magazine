<p>SAKA Museum at AYANA Bali has opened its first temporary exhibition with <strong>KAMASAN: The Noble Tradition of Balinese Painting</strong>, an expansive look at one of Bali’s most distinctive artistic traditions. Running through May 30, 2027, the exhibition brings together more than 30 works spanning the late 19th century to today, including ritual cloths, palm-leaf works, an astrological calendar and paintings preserved by the families of Kamasan masters. Rooted in the village of Kamasan and closely connected to Balinese-Hindu ritual, the tradition is recognizable for its highly codified figures, elaborate linework and connection to the visual language of wayang kulit.</p>
<p>Rather than treating Kamasan as a historical art form frozen in time, the exhibition places classical works alongside contemporary artists who are pushing its visual vocabulary into new territory. Curated by Farah Wardani and Made Chandra, the exhibition explores hereditary master painters, the women who have sustained the tradition, and a younger generation using Kamasan imagery to consider identity, ecology, technology and collective memory. The result is a portrait of a tradition that has survived not by remaining unchanged, but by continually finding ways to speak to the present.</p>
<p><strong>The Curated Take:</strong><br/>KAMASAN offers a particularly meaningful way to encounter Balinese culture because the paintings are inseparable from the beliefs, rituals, stories and communities that shaped them. The decision to give contemporary practitioners equal footing with historic works also makes this more than a survey of traditional art. It invites visitors to consider what cultural preservation actually looks like when a living tradition is allowed to evolve.</p>
