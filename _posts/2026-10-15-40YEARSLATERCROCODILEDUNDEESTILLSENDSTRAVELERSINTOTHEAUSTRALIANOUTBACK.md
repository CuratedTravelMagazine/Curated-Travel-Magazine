<p>Forty years after <em>Crocodile Dundee</em> arrived in U.S. cinemas, the film’s influence on Australian tourism is still visible in the travelers who follow its trail into the Northern Territory. The 1986 hit transformed the landscapes of Australia into a global backdrop, with Kakadu National Park among its most recognizable locations. The film became the second-highest-grossing movie at the U.S. box office that year, behind <em>Top Gun</em>, while international visitor numbers to Australia doubled to more than two million annually by 1990. Its longer legacy may be the way it helped establish what is now known as set jetting, turning places seen on screen into destinations worth experiencing in person.</p>
<p>Kakadu remains the center of the <em>Crocodile Dundee</em> story, but today’s travelers can experience the landscape beyond the movie set. Self-guided itineraries and 4WD tours revisit locations associated with the film, while Yellow Water cruises offer encounters with saltwater crocodiles, waterbirds and other wildlife on the billabong. Accommodation ranges from the distinctive Crocodile Hotel to Cooinda Lodge, with the wider national park providing access to waterfalls, gorges, wetlands and Indigenous cultural landscapes that give the region its identity beyond its Hollywood connection.</p>
<p><strong>The Curated Take:</strong> The enduring “Dundee effect” is an early example of something that has become increasingly powerful in travel: a story can create curiosity about a place, but the landscape has to deliver an experience worth traveling for. In Kakadu, the film provides the starting point, while the wildlife, scale and cultural depth of the Northern Territory give travelers a reason to keep exploring long after the movie reference has faded.</p>
